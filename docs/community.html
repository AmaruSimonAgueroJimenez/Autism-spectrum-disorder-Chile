5.1 What REM measures

REM (Resúmenes Estadísticos Mensuales), the monthly statistical summaries of the public network, contains aggregated records of care activity: each row is a facility, a month and a service code. A mental-health programme entry does not necessarily represent a new diagnosis, a programme discharge does not describe the outcome of a cohort of entries, and no figure corresponds to unique persons nationally. These counts do not estimate incidence, prevalence, retention or individual times to diagnosis. The population rates presented below are rates of recorded entries, an indicator of access and activity in the public system.

The extraction reads the canonical Series A files and applies each year’s dictionaries. The generic pervasive developmental disorder (PDD) codes of 2017–2020 are kept separate from the specific codes available from 2021. The Methods page details the reading rules; the table below summarises them.

Form Total and sex Caveat
A05, mental health Col01: total; Col02: males; Col03: females Ages start at Col04, in pairs by sex, up to Col37
A03, ASD screening sections Col01: males; Col02: females From 2023, Col03 and Col04 are Indigenous peoples and migrants; the total of a row requires both sex columns
A28, rehabilitation codes with an ASD condition Col01: both sexes Entry sections are kept separate; they are not added up as unique persons

total_known adds up only the rows that have a complete total according to that rule. It may be a partial sum when fields are missing; it does not automatically replace the national total. Sums of observed cells are identified as such.

5.2.3 Entry rates by population

The rates use the reported age and sex cells (Col04 to Col37) as numerator and the INE (national statistics institute) population of the year as denominator; they are age-standardised to the WHO world standard population and accompanied by 95% gamma intervals. They are rates of recorded entries, not of persons or of new cases.

5.7.2 Commune-level correlation

At the commune scale the comparison is more demanding: REM assigns entries to the facility commune and GRD to the commune of residence, so a commune without a facility reporting the code has no REM entries even if its residents are hospitalised. 6 Synthesis

  • Mental-health programme entries with an autism code rose from about 2,100 in 2021 to about 11,800 in 2024, that is, from 14 to 81 age-standardised entries per 100 000 population, with an average annual growth close to 70% (95% CI: 49 to 90) and a male:female ratio that fell from 3.5:1 to 2.2:1.
  • About half of the entries correspond to children under 10 years, with a growing proportion of adolescents and adults; specific autism displaces Asperger and unspecified PDD as the coding category.
  • Regional entry rates vary more than sixfold between regions (Ñuble and Atacama versus Magallanes, Tarapacá and the Metropolitan Region); growth occurs in the stable panel of facilities and also through new reporters.
  • Screening in 18-month check-ups shows that the recorded administration of the M-CHAT R/F is far below the number of check-ups and that about a third of children with medium or high risk require referral after Part 2; no ratio from this source is equivalent to a population positivity rate.
  • Territorial coherence between outpatient entries (REM) and hospitalisations (GRD) is weak by region and by commune, and only reaches moderate values among communes with sufficient volumes in both sources, which underlines that each source describes a different part of contact with the system.

## What REM measures

REM (Resúmenes Estadísticos Mensuales), the monthly statistical summaries of the public network, contains **aggregated records of care activity**: each row is a facility, a month and a service code. A mental-health programme entry does not necessarily represent a new diagnosis, a programme discharge does not describe the outcome of a cohort of entries, and no figure corresponds to unique persons nationally. These counts do not estimate incidence, prevalence, retention or individual times to diagnosis. The population rates presented below are **rates of recorded entries**, an indicator of access and activity in the public system.

The extraction reads the canonical Series A files and applies each year's dictionaries. The generic pervasive developmental disorder (PDD) codes of 2017–2020 are kept separate from the specific codes available from 2021. The [Methods](methods.html) page details the reading rules; the table below summarises them.

| Form | Total and sex | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| A05, mental health | `Col01`: total; `Col02`: males; `Col03`: females | Ages start at `Col04`, in pairs by sex, up to `Col37` |
| A03, ASD screening sections | `Col01`: males; `Col02`: females | From 2023, `Col03` and `Col04` are Indigenous peoples and migrants; the total of a row requires both sex columns |
| A28, rehabilitation codes with an ASD condition | `Col01`: both sexes | Entry sections are kept separate; they are not added up as unique persons |

`total_known` adds up only the rows that have a complete total according to that rule. It may be a partial sum when fields are missing; it does not automatically replace the national total. Sums of observed cells are identified as such.

### Commune-level correlation

At the commune scale the comparison is more demanding: REM assigns entries to the facility commune and GRD to the commune of residence, so a commune without a facility reporting the code has no REM entries even if its residents are hospitalised. The main analysis uses the continental communes with at least one reporting facility; as sensitivity analyses, it adds the communes with at least 20 entries and 20 hospitalised persons, where rates are more stable, and all continental communes with zero imputed to those that did not report.

# Synthesis

- Mental-health programme entries with an autism code rose from about 2,100 in 2021 to about 11,800 in 2024, that is, from 14 to 81 age-standardised entries per 100 000 population, with an average annual growth close to 70% (95% CI: 49 to 90) and a male:female ratio that fell from 3.5:1 to 2.2:1.
- About half of the entries correspond to children under 10 years, with a growing proportion of adolescents and adults; specific autism displaces Asperger and unspecified PDD as the coding category.
- Regional entry rates vary more than sixfold between regions (Ñuble and Atacama versus Magallanes, Tarapacá and the Metropolitan Region); growth occurs in the stable panel of facilities and also through new reporters.
- Screening in 18-month check-ups shows that the recorded administration of the M-CHAT R/F is far below the number of check-ups and that about a third of children with medium or high risk require referral after Part 2; no ratio from this source is equivalent to a population positivity rate.
- Territorial coherence between outpatient entries (REM) and hospitalisations (GRD) is weak by region and by commune, and only reaches moderate values among communes with sufficient volumes in both sources, which underlines that each source describes a different part of contact with the system.

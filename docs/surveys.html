7.1 What the surveys measure

ENDIDE 2022 and ENCAVI 2023–2024 are the only sources in this report that estimate something about a population instead of counting administrative records. Every other chapter counts rows that a service produced: an episode, a programme entry, a stock under follow-up, a school enrolment. Here the unit is a sampled person, the numbers are design-weighted estimates carrying sampling error, and the domains are defined by the wording of a questionnaire item rather than by a diagnostic code.

Three things follow, and they hold for every figure in this chapter. First, each estimate is the weighted proportion of a survey domain whose respondent reported autism or a received diagnosis; it measures reporting, not clinical ascertainment, and no item in either survey applies a diagnostic instrument. Second, every estimate has a confidence interval, and an estimate without its interval is not a result. Third, a survey estimate and an administrative count are not the same quantity: they are never joined into one series, and where both appear in a figure they sit on separate axes with the difference stated.

7.1.1 The sample design of each survey

Both surveys are estimated with their published complex design: the expansion weight as the probability weight, the published stratum as the variance stratum and the published primary sampling unit as the variance cluster, with Taylor linearisation and logit intervals on a t distribution whose degrees of freedom are the clusters minus the strata.

The wording is the definition, and it differs between the two surveys and between the two ENDIDE modules. ENDIDE asks a household informant about a condition from a battery of health conditions, for adults as self-report or by proxy and for children through the main caregiver, and then asks the caregiver — and only the caregiver — whether a physician said so. ENCAVI asks the selected person aged 15 or over, in a personal interview, whether they have been diagnosed, with an interviewer note that a traditional medical diagnosis is meant, and separately whether they have received treatment for it. A reported condition, a caregiver-reported physician confirmation and a self-reported diagnosis are three different quantities; the report keeps them apart and never averages them.

7.8 What this source cannot support

  • Prevalence or incidence. The items ask what a person or a caregiver reports, not what a clinical assessment finds. The estimate is of reported recognition in a household population; a study that wanted prevalence would need an ascertainment instrument, and neither survey carries one.
  • A trend. One cross-section each, two instruments, two field periods. Nothing in this chapter is a rate of change, and no year-on-year comparison is defensible from these two points.
  • A regional, service or communal figure. No domain below the national level is estimated, so nothing here can enter the territorial analyses of the report.
  • A cascade from report to service. The confirmation and treatment items describe what respondents said, not encounters that can be matched to the records in the other chapters. The sources are not person-linked and no path from a survey respondent to an administrative record exists.
  • A denominator for the administrative counts. The weighted persons of a survey domain do not cover the same population, the same period or the same definition as any register in this report, and the study describes them as a benchmark of order of magnitude only.
  • The small cells. The estimates flagged imprecise — counted, named and marked above — are an editorial caution rather than an exclusion: each is printed with its interval, hatched or left open on every figure that carries it, and never quoted alone. Under the revised rule that caution covers every ENCAVI age band, so no age-specific ENCAVI figure should be read as a measurement.

## What the surveys measure

ENDIDE 2022 and ENCAVI 2023–2024 are the only sources in this report that **estimate something about a
population** instead of counting administrative records. Every other chapter counts rows that a service
produced: an episode, a programme entry, a stock under follow-up, a school enrolment. Here the unit is a
**sampled person**, the numbers are **design-weighted estimates** carrying sampling error, and the domains
are defined by the wording of a questionnaire item rather than by a diagnostic code.

Three things follow, and they hold for every figure in this chapter. First, each estimate is the weighted
proportion of a survey domain whose respondent **reported** autism or a received diagnosis; it measures
reporting, not clinical ascertainment, and no item in either survey applies a diagnostic instrument. Second,
every estimate has a confidence interval, and an estimate without its interval is not a result. Third, a
survey estimate and an administrative count are **not the same quantity**: they are never joined into one
series, and where both appear in a figure they sit on separate axes with the difference stated.

weight, the published stratum as the variance stratum and the published primary sampling unit as the
variance cluster, with Taylor linearisation and logit intervals on a *t* distribution whose degrees of
freedom are the clusters minus the strata.

The wording is the definition, and it differs between the two surveys and between the two ENDIDE modules.
ENDIDE asks a household informant about a **condition** from a battery of health conditions, for adults as
self-report or by proxy and for children through the main caregiver, and then asks the caregiver — and only
the caregiver — whether a physician said so. ENCAVI asks the selected person aged 15 or over, in a personal
interview, whether they have **been diagnosed**, with an interviewer note that a traditional medical
diagnosis is meant, and separately whether they have received treatment for it. A reported condition, a
caregiver-reported physician confirmation and a self-reported diagnosis are three different quantities; the
report keeps them apart and never averages them.

## What this source cannot support

- **Prevalence or incidence.** The items ask what a person or a caregiver reports, not what a clinical
  assessment finds. The estimate is of reported recognition in a household population; a study that wanted
  prevalence would need an ascertainment instrument, and neither survey carries one.
- **A trend.** One cross-section each, two instruments, two field periods. Nothing in this chapter is a rate
  of change, and no year-on-year comparison is defensible from these two points.
- **A regional, service or communal figure.** No domain below the national level is estimated, so nothing
  here can enter the territorial analyses of the report.
- **A cascade from report to service.** The confirmation and treatment items describe what respondents said,
  not encounters that can be matched to the records in the other chapters. The sources are not person-linked
  and no path from a survey respondent to an administrative record exists.
- **A denominator for the administrative counts.** The weighted persons of a survey domain do not cover the
  same population, the same period or the same definition as any register in this report, and the study
  describes them as a benchmark of order of magnitude only.
- **The small cells.** The estimates flagged imprecise — counted, named and marked above — are an editorial
  caution rather than an exclusion: each is printed with its interval, hatched or left open on every figure that
  carries it, and never quoted alone. Under the revised rule that caution covers every ENCAVI age band, so no
  age-specific ENCAVI figure should be read as a measurement.

## Reproducibility

The estimates are produced by `study/pipeline/04_surveys.py`, which reads the two microdata files whose
SHA-256 digests are recorded above, applies each survey's published weight, stratum and cluster variables and
writes `survey_estimates.csv`; `survey_estimates_verified.csv` is the same table after verification and
`survey_domains_enriched_verified.csv` adds the two precision flags and the text of the revised rule.
`fig3c_surveys.csv` selects the four headline domains and fixes their order. This chapter recomputes every
table and figure from those files and from the corpus tables `ST5_survey_items.csv`, `E23_surveys_detail.csv`,
`E75_survey_estimates_full.csv`, `E26_cross_source.csv`, `E26b_sex_ratio_multisource.csv`, `T1_sources.csv`,
`T8_controls.csv` and `ST7_provenance.csv`. Every quantity in the tables, the figures and the running text —
the two precision thresholds, the age-band lists, the counts of flagged cells and the reporting bases of the
administrative panel included — is computed from those files at render time. A handful of fixed labels are
written out: the year of the code-set break marked on the comparison figure, the field periods of the two
surveys and the age at which the network screens. Every quantity is read from a table,
and where the text rounds a computed value into words the computed value is printed beside it. The microdata
themselves are not redistributed: only the estimate tables are versioned.

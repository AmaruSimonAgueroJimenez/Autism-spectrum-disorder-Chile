6.1 What the education records count

The education sources count school registrations, not people diagnosed and not persons in the population. A student appears in the Programa de Integración Escolar (PIE, the school integration programme) when a state-funded establishment registers that student under a permanent special educational need and receives the associated subsidy; a student appears in the special-school series when enrolled in an Escuela Especial de Autismo. Both are annual stocks of the school register: one row per school year, never a count of new cases, never a count of unique persons across years, and never a denominator-bearing rate. The JUNAEB school survey counts something different again — a caregiver’s answer to a checklist item in selected school cohorts. Nothing on this page is prevalence or incidence; what rises here is administrative and educational recognition inside the school system.

The strict and Asperger categories sum exactly to the harmonised series in every year the split is published, which is what the check column shows. From 2024 onward the split is not published at all, so the strict series simply stops: the harmonised series is the only one that runs the whole period. The three share columns carry a second warning, which belongs here and not in a closing list: their denominators are not stable. Total PIE enrolment falls 11.1% in 2020 — from 385,995 to 343,059 students of every special educational need — and rises 15.7% again in 2021. Of the 1.43 percentage-point rise in the harmonised share between 2019 and 2020, 0.68 points — 48% of it — is the denominator contracting, with the numerator held at its earlier value. Total PIE applicants then falls 4.7% in 2025. Every share on this page is read with the movement of its own denominator beside it.

Two definitions coexist because the register itself carries two categories. Strict autism is the permanent need labelled Trastorno del Espectro Autista (P); Autism–Asperger is a separate permanent need kept from the pre-DSM-5 nomenclature. Reporting only the strict category would drop every student registered under the older label; reporting only their sum would hide that the two categories behave very differently. Both are therefore reported, and the harmonised series — their sum — is the one used wherever a single number is needed, because it is the only definition the later source publishes.

# The provenance file records each definition in the language of the source document; the published
# corpus table E76 carries the English definition of the same series, and is used for display.
# E76 carries one English definition per published series, but `pie_harmonised` is published by two
# documents under two different definitions, so the definition has to be resolved per (series, document)
# and not per series: mapping by series alone printed the Apuntes 60 definition against the SINACES years.

But the audit marks design inference as not defensible in every single level-year, including the weighted ones, because the published files contain no stratum, no primary sampling unit and no school identifier: the standard errors below treat students as independent, which ignores clustering within schools and is therefore optimistic. None of these level-years is used as central evidence anywhere in the study.

Five weaknesses are worth naming plainly, because every number above inherits at least one of them.

The unit is a registration, not a person. A student registered in PIE in two consecutive years is two rows in two annual stocks. Nothing in the published aggregates permits following an individual, and no figure here is a count of distinct persons over the period.

The register measures eligibility and provision as much as recognition. A registration requires a diagnosis, but it also requires an establishment with a PIE, a professional to complete the assessment, and either an available Decree 170 place or an exceptional authorisation. The rising share of exceptional entries is direct evidence that the supply constraint binds. Growth in this series therefore mixes clinical recognition, administrative capacity and the financial incentive attached to the subsidy, in proportions the data cannot separate.

The denominators do not match the numerators. Total PIE enrolment counts students with any special educational need in state-funded establishments; the population denominator counts residents of all ages; the survey denominators count records in a file. None of them is the population of school-age children in Chile, so none of the percentages on this page is a prevalence, and the shares across the source switch are not taken over the same base.

Every figure and table on this page is computed in the chunk that displays it, from tables tracked in git: docs/study/data/education_summary_year.csv, pie_source_provenance.csv, junaeb_denominator_audit.csv, coverage_layers_year.csv and models_summary.csv, together with the published corpus tables ST6_junaeb_items.csv, E76_education_pie_full.csv, S12_junaeb_sex_level.csv and E79_reproduction_controls_by_family.csv. No value is transcribed by hand and nothing is read from a geospatial file, because no tracked education table carries a region or a commune.

The prior extraction is documented in the provenance table itself: each published PIE, SINACES or special-school value keeps the document it was read from, the physical page, the caption of the table on that page, the definition recorded beside it and the SHA-256 of the file, and is flagged as printed or as computed from printed cells. The survey side keeps the microdata file, its encoding, its dictionary and questionnaire, the exact variable that carries the ASD item, the values observed in it and the weight column, for every level and year. The growth models are read from models_summary.csv with their residual degrees of freedom, dispersion and Durbin–Watson statistic attached, so the weakness of a log-linear fit on seven points stays visible beside its interval.

The extension this source most needs is not more years but more columns: a regional and age breakdown of the PIE register, a sex split published every year rather than once, an establishment count for the special schools, and a survey file that publishes the sampling design alongside the weight. Until then the education record can be counted accurately and cannot be disaggregated at all.

## What the education records count

The education sources count **school registrations, not people diagnosed and not persons in the
population**. A student appears in the Programa de Integración Escolar (PIE, the school integration
programme) when a state-funded establishment registers that student under a permanent special
educational need and receives the associated subsidy; a student appears in the special-school series
when enrolled in an Escuela Especial de Autismo. Both are **annual stocks** of the school register:
one row per school year, never a count of new cases, never a count of unique persons across years,
and never a denominator-bearing rate. The JUNAEB school survey counts something different again — a
caregiver's answer to a checklist item in selected school cohorts. Nothing on this page is prevalence
or incidence; what rises here is administrative and educational recognition inside the school system.

### What the provenance table records

```{python}

# The provenance file records each definition in the language of the source document; the published
# corpus table E76 carries the English definition of the same series, and is used for display.
# E76 carries one English definition per published series, but `pie_harmonised` is published by two
```

The third denominator, the resident population, is shown once and then left alone. A school
registration divided by the population of every age is not an educational rate, not a prevalence and
not comparable with the health-record rates elsewhere in this report, which have residents as both
unit and denominator. It appears here only so that the order of magnitude of the school series can be
placed beside the other sources, and it carries the same warning wherever it is used.

## The school survey: what a caregiver checklist can carry

The Encuesta de Vulnerabilidad Estudiantil (EVE), the annual school-feeding and vulnerability survey

one whose weaknesses need stating most carefully, because it is the only one that looks like it could
produce a prevalence estimate — and it cannot.

Five weaknesses are worth naming plainly, because every number above inherits at least one of them.

**The unit is a registration, not a person.** A student registered in PIE in two consecutive years is
two rows in two annual stocks. Nothing in the published aggregates permits following an individual,
and no figure here is a count of distinct persons over the period.

**The register measures eligibility and provision as much as recognition.** A registration requires a
diagnosis, but it also requires an establishment with a PIE, a professional to complete the
assessment, and either an available Decree 170 place or an exceptional authorisation. The rising
share of exceptional entries is direct evidence that the supply constraint binds. Growth in this
series therefore mixes clinical recognition, administrative capacity and the financial incentive
attached to the subsidy, in proportions the data cannot separate.

**The denominators do not match the numerators.** Total PIE enrolment counts students with any
special educational need in state-funded establishments; the population denominator counts residents
of all ages; the survey denominators count records in a file. None of them is the population of
school-age children in Chile, so none of the percentages on this page is a prevalence, and the shares
across the source switch are not taken over the same base.

## Reproducibility

Every figure and table on this page is computed in the chunk that displays it, from tables tracked in
git: `docs/study/data/education_summary_year.csv`, `pie_source_provenance.csv`,
`junaeb_denominator_audit.csv`, `coverage_layers_year.csv` and `models_summary.csv`, together with the
published corpus tables `ST6_junaeb_items.csv`, `E76_education_pie_full.csv`,
`S12_junaeb_sex_level.csv` and `E79_reproduction_controls_by_family.csv`. That is the whole input list:
the chapter reads nothing it does not use, so `pie_enriched_context_verified.csv` and `fig3a_pie.csv`,
which restate the same PIE series in other shapes, are not loaded here and are not claimed as sources
of anything on the page. No value is transcribed by hand and nothing is read from a geospatial file,
because no tracked education table carries a region or a commune.

The prior extraction is documented in the provenance table itself: each published PIE, SINACES or
special-school value keeps the document it was read from, the physical page, the caption of the table
on that page, the definition recorded beside it and the SHA-256 of the file, and is flagged as
printed or as computed from printed cells. The survey side keeps the microdata file, its encoding,
its dictionary and questionnaire, the exact variable that carries the ASD item, the values observed
in it and the weight column, for every level and year. The growth models are read from
`models_summary.csv` with their residual degrees of freedom, dispersion and Durbin–Watson statistic
attached, so the weakness of a log-linear fit on seven points stays visible beside its interval.

The extension this source most needs is not more years but more columns: a regional and age breakdown
of the PIE register, a sex split published every year rather than once, an establishment count for
the special schools, and a survey file that publishes the sampling design alongside the weight. Until
then the education record can be counted accurately and cannot be disaggregated at all.

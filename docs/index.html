This report describes how autism is recognised in the routine administrative records of Chile’s public health and education systems. It brings together every result the study produced, organised by what the result is about rather than by when it was computed.

Nothing here is prevalence or incidence. Every number is a count of hospital episodes, community programme entries, follow-up stocks or school enrolments, presented with its unit, its denominator and its coverage. The rise these records show is consistent with greater detection, greater demand and changes in coding during the period of Law 21.545; it does not demonstrate a change in how often autism occurs.

0.1 What the report contains

Methods. Sources, case definitions, units, denominators, standardisation, intervals, trends, spatial analysis, the rules for reading the monthly returns, quality controls, biases and limitations. It is the common reference for everything that follows.

Results. The principal findings: four panel figures and the nine supplementary figures that support them, each drawn at render time by the code printed with it.

Hospital recognition. The hospital discharge records in depth: magnitude and trend, age-standardised rates, sex and age, F84 subcategories, the diagnostic position of the code, episode characteristics, co-diagnoses, readmission, territory and spatial autocorrelation, and the reconstruction of which diagnoses precede the first observed autism record.

Community care. The monthly statistical returns of the public network: entries to and discharges from mental-health programmes with an autism code, their population rates, age, sex, regions, communes and facilities, screening in well-child check-ups, rehabilitation entries, and the ecological comparison between the two sources.

Result tables and extended plates hold the complete material, including the results that the main chapters summarise. Reproducibility explains how to rebuild every number in this report from the repository.

Hospitalisations and community programme entries with an autism code have grown rapidly since 2021, more in females than in males, with the greatest intensity between 5 and 14 years of age and with strong territorial heterogeneity. In the 2021–2024 hospital trajectories, the first autism record almost always occurs with the code in a secondary position, and only a minority has observable prior hospitalisations. Those earlier codes do not show that they were ruled out as differential diagnoses: they may be comorbidities, symptoms or independent reasons for admission.

0.3 How this report was built

Every table and figure is recalculated when the report is rendered; none is pasted in. The chapters read the aggregated tables in output_files/consolidacion/ and docs/study/data/, both versioned in git, so the whole report can be rebuilt from a clone without the source microdata. The extraction from the original microdata runs separately, through the scripts documented in Reproducibility.

The study itself was written over several rounds of revision, which added results without ever changing one that had already been computed — a claim checked rather than asserted, by hashing every result table of every round and comparing them pairwise. This report presents the union of all of them as a single body of work. Where a result comes from a different observation window or an earlier case definition, the table says so on the spot.

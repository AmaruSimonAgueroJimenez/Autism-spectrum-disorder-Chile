This report has two parts. Part A describes the epidemiology of hospitalisations carrying an autism spectrum disorder (ASD) code in GRD (Grupos Relacionados por el Diagnóstico), the public-hospital discharge database grouped by diagnosis-related groups: how many there are, how they evolve, who has them, where they live, what the episodes look like and which other diagnoses accompany them. Part B reconstructs the coding trajectories: which hospital diagnoses appear before the first observed autism record, and in which position it is recorded. Definitions, denominators and methods are in Methods; here only the caveats needed to read each result are repeated.

The main definition is operational ASD (F84.0, F84.1, F84.5, F84.8 and F84.9 in any of the 35 diagnostic positions). Rates are expressed per 100 000 person-years of the INE (national statistics institute) population and are age-standardised to the WHO world standard population. No rate in this report is an incidence or prevalence of autism: they are rates of hospitalisations, or of hospitalised persons, carrying the code in public facilities with the GRD system.

Each annual file was read in full and the records with any F84 code were kept after removing exact duplicates. A record is an episode of hospitalisation or of major ambulatory surgery. Identifiers allow distinct persons to be counted within each year and within each identifier period (2019–2020 and 2021–2024), but not across the two periods.

def pooled_los(frame, keys):
    """Exact quantiles are not additive across roles; means and totals are pooled, quantiles come from the widest stratum."""

7 Summary

  • Hospitalisations with an ASD code in the public GRD system rose from about 16 to about 59 per 100 000 person-years (age-standardised) between 2019 and 2024, with average annual growth above 50% between 2021 and 2024, concentrated in records with the code in a secondary position and in category F84.0. The growth is consistent with greater detection, greater demand and coding changes; it does not demonstrate greater occurrence.
  • The male:female ratio of the rates fell from 3:1 to 2:1 because of the faster increase in hospitalisations among females; the highest rates are observed between 5 and 14 years of age.
  • Episodes are mostly hospitalisations for other causes (emergency, paediatrics, dental and ear, nose and throat surgery, epilepsy, respiratory infections, psychiatric causes); in-hospital case fatality is low and the longest stays correspond to episodes with autism as the principal diagnosis.
  • There is marked territorial heterogeneity, with positive spatial autocorrelation and high-high clusters around Ñuble and Biobío, which describe the organisation of the network and coding practice.
  • In the 2021–2024 trajectories, the great majority of first records occur with the code in a secondary position and a minority have observable previous hospitalisations; the recorded prior diagnoses are mainly epilepsy, language and developmental disorders and conduct problems, without the sequence demonstrating differential diagnoses that were ruled out.

# Part B. Hospital diagnostic trajectories

## B1. Question and definition

**Which hospital diagnoses appear before the first observed autism record, and when is it recorded as principal or secondary?**

The primary analysis uses the operational definition F84.0, F84.1, F84.5, F84.8 and F84.9 in any of the 35 diagnostic positions. All discharges of those identifiers are retrieved, including those without F84. Comparisons are also made with specific F84.0 and with historical F84.

The index date is the **first observed discharge** with the selected definition. Age refers to admission in that episode. The date of clinical diagnosis is unknown, and autism may have been diagnosed earlier, in outpatient care or outside the available period.

`DIAGNOSTICO1` is used operationally as the principal diagnosis and `DIAGNOSTICO2–35` as secondary diagnoses. A prior diagnosis may be a comorbidity, a symptom, an independent reason for admission or an alternative diagnosis. **The sequence of codes does not demonstrate that these were clinical differentials that were ruled out.**

# Summary

- Hospitalisations with an ASD code in the public GRD system rose from about 16 to about 59 per 100 000 person-years (age-standardised) between 2019 and 2024, with average annual growth above 50% between 2021 and 2024, concentrated in records with the code in a secondary position and in category F84.0. The growth is consistent with greater detection, greater demand and coding changes; it does not demonstrate greater occurrence.
- The male:female ratio of the rates fell from 3:1 to 2:1 because of the faster increase in hospitalisations among females; the highest rates are observed between 5 and 14 years of age.
- Episodes are mostly hospitalisations for other causes (emergency, paediatrics, dental and ear, nose and throat surgery, epilepsy, respiratory infections, psychiatric causes); in-hospital case fatality is low and the longest stays correspond to episodes with autism as the principal diagnosis.
- There is marked territorial heterogeneity, with positive spatial autocorrelation and high-high clusters around Ñuble and Biobío, which describe the organisation of the network and coding practice.
- In the 2021–2024 trajectories, the great majority of first records occur with the code in a secondary position and a minority have observable previous hospitalisations; the recorded prior diagnoses are mainly epilepsy, language and developmental disorders and conduct problems, without the sequence demonstrating differential diagnoses that were ruled out.
